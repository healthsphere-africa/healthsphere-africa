Malaria

Understanding malaria — how it spreads, what it does inside the body, how it is diagnosed, treated and prevented.

Explore this article 1-Minute Summary How It Works Symptoms Diagnosis Treatment Prevention Danger Signs Myths Knowledge Check

⏱️ Malaria in One Minute

  • Malaria is an infectious disease caused by Plasmodium parasites.
  • It is mainly transmitted through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito.
  • Common symptoms include fever, chills, headache, weakness, muscle aches, nausea and sometimes vomiting.
  • Malaria can become severe, particularly in young children, pregnant people and people with certain health vulnerabilities.
  • Testing is important because many illnesses can cause fever.
  • Malaria is treatable, and early appropriate treatment reduces the risk of severe disease.
  • Mosquito-control measures and other prevention strategies help reduce transmission.

🦟 What Is Malaria?

Malaria is an infectious disease caused by parasites of the Plasmodium group. Several Plasmodium species can infect humans.

Transmission usually occurs when an infected female Anopheles mosquito bites a person and introduces malaria parasites into the bloodstream.

Malaria is particularly important in tropical and subtropical regions, including many parts of Africa.

🧬 How Malaria Works in the Body

One of the most useful ways to understand malaria is to follow what happens after the parasite enters the body.

1
The mosquito bite

An infected mosquito introduces malaria parasites into the person's bloodstream.

2
The liver stage

The parasites travel to the liver, where they develop and multiply. This stage can occur before symptoms begin.

3
Red blood cells become involved

Parasites enter red blood cells and multiply. Infected cells eventually rupture, releasing more parasites.

4
The immune system responds

The body's immune response contributes to symptoms such as fever, chills and general weakness.

5
Repeated infection of red blood cells

Continued parasite multiplication can damage red blood cells and contribute to anaemia and other complications.

🔄 How Does Malaria Spread?

The usual transmission cycle involves mosquitoes and humans.

Step 1 An infected mosquito bites a person.
Step 2 The parasite develops inside the person's body.
Step 3 Another mosquito bites an infected person and can acquire parasites.
Step 4 The parasite develops inside the mosquito.
Step 5 The infected mosquito can transmit malaria during a later bite.

Malaria is not normally spread through ordinary social contact such as shaking hands, sitting beside someone or sharing a meal.

🤒 Signs and Symptoms

Symptoms can vary from person to person. Common symptoms include:

  • Fever
  • Chills or feeling cold
  • Headache
  • Weakness or unusual tiredness
  • Muscle or joint aches
  • Nausea
  • Vomiting
  • Sometimes diarrhoea

Why does malaria cause fever?

Fever is partly related to the body's immune response to malaria parasites and changes associated with infected red blood cells.

Why can malaria cause weakness?

Infection can produce significant inflammation and, in some cases, destruction of red blood cells. Anaemia can also contribute to weakness.

⚠️ Who Is More Vulnerable to Severe Malaria?

The risk of severe illness is influenced by age, pregnancy, immunity, access to prompt treatment and other individual factors.

  • Young children, particularly those in malaria-endemic areas
  • Pregnant people
  • People with limited immunity to malaria
  • People who develop delayed or inadequate treatment
Important: A person who initially appears to have mild illness can become seriously ill. Worsening symptoms should not be ignored.

🧪 How Is Malaria Diagnosed?

Because fever can have many causes, malaria should ideally be confirmed with an appropriate diagnostic test where testing is available.

Common approaches include:

  • Malaria rapid diagnostic tests
  • Microscopic examination of blood films
  • Other laboratory methods in appropriate settings

The healthcare professional may also assess symptoms, medical history and other possible causes of illness.

💊 Treatment and Management

Malaria treatment depends on factors such as the parasite species, severity of illness, age, pregnancy status, previous treatment and the local pattern of antimalarial drug resistance.

Appropriate antimalarial treatment should therefore be selected according to current clinical guidance and assessment by a qualified healthcare professional.

Do not guess the treatment. Not every fever is malaria, and different forms of malaria may require different treatment approaches. Testing and professional assessment are important.

🛡️ How Can Malaria Be Prevented?

🛏️ Insecticide-treated nets Sleeping under an appropriate insecticide-treated mosquito net can reduce exposure to malaria-transmitting mosquitoes.
🏠 Reduce mosquito exposure Use appropriate mosquito-control measures around homes and sleeping areas.
🌿 Environmental control Where practical, reducing mosquito breeding opportunities can contribute to vector control.
🏥 Preventive care Follow recommended malaria prevention programmes, especially for groups such as pregnant people and young children.

🚨 Danger Signs: When Urgent Care Is Needed

Severe malaria can be life-threatening. Seek urgent medical attention if someone with suspected or confirmed malaria develops serious or rapidly worsening symptoms.

  • Confusion or unusual difficulty staying awake
  • Seizures
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Repeated vomiting or inability to drink
  • Severe weakness or deterioration
  • Other signs of serious illness
Do not wait for symptoms to become severe before seeking care.

👶 Malaria in Children

Children can become seriously ill from malaria, particularly in areas where malaria transmission is common.

A child with fever, unusual weakness, poor feeding, repeated vomiting, altered behaviour, difficulty breathing or other concerning symptoms should be assessed promptly by a healthcare professional.

🤰 Malaria in Pregnancy

Malaria during pregnancy can affect both the pregnant person and the developing baby. Prevention, appropriate testing and treatment are therefore particularly important.

Pregnant people should follow the malaria prevention and treatment recommendations provided by their healthcare team and local health programme.

🩸 Malaria and Anaemia

Malaria can contribute to anaemia because malaria parasites infect red blood cells and infected cells may be destroyed.

Repeated or severe infections can therefore contribute to significant reductions in red blood cells, which may worsen tiredness, weakness and other symptoms.

💡 Did You Know?

  • Malaria parasites have different stages during their life cycle.
  • The malaria parasite can involve both liver cells and red blood cells during infection.
  • Malaria prevention involves controlling exposure to malaria-transmitting mosquitoes as well as other public-health measures.
  • Fever does not automatically mean a person has malaria; other infections and conditions can also cause fever.

❌ Myths & Misconceptions

MYTH: Every fever is malaria.
FACT: Many illnesses can cause fever. Appropriate testing helps determine whether malaria is present.
MYTH: Malaria spreads simply by being around someone who has it.
FACT: Malaria is mainly transmitted through infected mosquitoes. Ordinary social contact does not normally transmit malaria.
MYTH: Once malaria symptoms improve, treatment can always be stopped.
FACT: Treatment should be taken exactly as prescribed by the healthcare professional.
MYTH: Malaria is always a mild illness.
FACT: Malaria can become severe and requires prompt attention when symptoms are concerning or worsening.

🫀 Malaria & the Human Body

🧫 Liver Important early stages of the parasite's development occur in liver cells.
🩸 Blood The parasite infects red blood cells and can contribute to their destruction.
🛡️ Immune System The immune response contributes to inflammation and many symptoms of malaria.
🧠 Brain Severe malaria can involve the nervous system and may cause serious neurological complications.
❤️ Whole Body Severe malaria can affect multiple organs and requires urgent medical management.

📌 Quick Facts

  • Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasites.
  • Infected Anopheles mosquitoes are the main vectors.
  • Blood testing can help confirm malaria.
  • Malaria can be prevented.
  • Malaria is treatable.
  • Severe malaria requires urgent medical care.
  • Not every fever is caused by malaria.
