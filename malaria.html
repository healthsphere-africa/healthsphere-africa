Malaria

Malaria is a serious disease caused by parasites that are transmitted to people through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. It is preventable and treatable, but it can become severe if treatment is delayed.

What is Malaria?

Malaria is an infectious disease caused by Plasmodium parasites. The disease is common in many tropical and subtropical areas, including parts of Africa.

Malaria is not spread by ordinary contact such as shaking hands, sharing food, or sitting beside someone who has the disease.

Common Symptoms

  • Fever
  • Chills or feeling cold
  • Headache
  • Body weakness or tiredness
  • Muscle and joint aches
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Sometimes diarrhoea

How Malaria is Diagnosed

Malaria is usually diagnosed by testing a person's blood for malaria parasites. Depending on the healthcare setting, this may include a malaria rapid diagnostic test or examination of a blood film.

Prevention

  • Sleep under insecticide-treated mosquito nets.
  • Reduce mosquito breeding sites around homes where possible.
  • Use recommended mosquito-control measures.
  • Seek advice about malaria prevention when travelling to high-risk areas.
  • Pregnant women and young children should receive appropriate preventive care according to local health guidance.

Treatment

Malaria treatment depends on the malaria parasite involved, the severity of the illness, the person's age and other factors. Antimalarial medicines should be selected according to current clinical guidance and the local pattern of malaria drug resistance.

Important: Do not delay medical care when malaria is suspected, especially in young children, pregnant women, older people, or anyone who is becoming seriously ill.

When to Seek Urgent Medical Care

Seek urgent medical attention if a person with suspected or confirmed malaria develops severe weakness, confusion, difficulty breathing, repeated vomiting, seizures, inability to drink, or other signs of serious illness.

Key Message

Malaria can be prevented and treated. Early testing and appropriate treatment can help reduce the risk of serious complications.
